Overview

What Session 1 covers

Every network — from a two-PC home setup to the internet itself — is built from the same three ingredients, arranged into a topology, and judged by the same four traits. Get this vocabulary locked in now; every later module assumes it.

  • Identify end devices, intermediary devices, and network media in any topology
  • Distinguish physical topology from logical topology
  • Compare LAN, WAN, and MAN by scope and ownership
  • Explain the four characteristics of a reliable network: fault tolerance, scalability, QoS, security
  • Describe current trends (BYOD, cloud, IoT) and name basic security threats/solutions at a high level

Building blocks

Network components

Three ingredients make up every network: devices that send or receive messages, devices that move traffic between them, and the media the data travels on.

How it looks

Topology: physical vs. logical

Two switches in different buildings connected by fiber are physically apart but may be on the same logical network (one VLAN, one IP subnet). The two views tell different stories.

Scope

LAN, WAN, MAN, and the Internet

How far a network reaches — and who owns it — determines everything from equipment choices to security requirements.

Applied

Worked examples

Think it through before you expand the answer.

Component ID

Name every piece

A small office has 3 laptops connecting wirelessly to an access point, which connects by cable to a switch, which connects to a router that reaches the ISP.

Show answer

End devices: the 3 laptops.

Intermediary devices: access point, switch, router.

Media: wireless (laptops ↔ AP), copper (AP ↔ switch ↔ router).

LAN vs. WAN

Which is it?

A company connects its Charleston office network to its Huntington office network using a link leased from a telecom provider.

Show answer

Answer: That leased link is a WAN — each office's internal network is its own LAN, and the WAN is what joins the two LANs across distance.

Reliable networks

Which trait is failing?

During a company-wide video call, everyone's audio keeps cutting out and video freezes, even though the internet connection itself hasn't gone down.

Show answer

Answer: Quality of Service (QoS) — real-time traffic like voice/video needs to be prioritized over other traffic (like a large file download) sharing the same link. Fault tolerance isn't the issue here since the connection is still up.

Topology

Physical vs. logical

Two PCs are in adjacent offices, both plugged into the same switch. A third PC is in a different building but is on the same VLAN and IP subnet. From the perspective of IP addressing, which two PCs are "closer"?

Show answer

Answer: The two PCs in the same VLAN/subnet — even though one is in a different building physically. IP operates on the logical topology, not the physical one. The PC in the other building is on the same logical network because it shares the same VLAN and subnet.

Security

Which CIA element?

A company's website goes offline during a DDoS attack. Customers can't access the site for 6 hours.

Show answer

Answer: Availability — the DDoS attack prevents legitimate users from accessing the service. Confidentiality and integrity aren't directly compromised; the data isn't exposed or altered, it's simply unreachable.

Media selection

Which media and why?

You need to connect two buildings that are 2 km apart on a college campus. The link must carry gigabit traffic and is running alongside high-voltage power lines.

Show answer

Answer: Fiber optic — it supports long distances (2 km is well within fiber range), provides high bandwidth, and is immune to electromagnetic interference from the power lines. Copper UTP is limited to ~100m and would be susceptible to EMI. Wireless could work but wouldn't reliably deliver gigabit speeds over that distance with interference.

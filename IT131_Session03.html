Module 4

Purpose & characteristics

The physical layer takes the frame handed down from Local Dispatch and encodes it into a signal that can actually travel: electrical pulses over copper, light pulses over fiber, or radio waves through the air. That's it — three media, no addressing, no logic, just transmission.

TermDefinition
EncodingHow a bit stream becomes a signal pattern on the medium (e.g., Manchester encoding).
Signaling methodHow 0s and 1s are represented physically — a voltage level, a light pulse, a radio wave modulation.
BandwidthThe theoretical maximum capacity of a medium, set by physics and standards. Measured in bps.
ThroughputThe actual measured transfer rate — always ≤ bandwidth, degraded by devices, errors, and overhead.
GoodputThroughput minus overhead — the usable data rate a user actually experiences.

Module 4

Copper cabling

Electrical signals — cheapest and most common, but vulnerable to interference. Twisting each pair of wires cancels out most of it, which is why it's called twisted pair in the first place.

MEDIUM 01

UTP / STP / Coax

Electrical pulses down twisted pairs — the default for every desk drop in the building.

STANDARD

On the range

Why does twisting help? Two wires carrying opposite signals, twisted together, generate opposing magnetic fields that cancel most external interference (EMI/RFI) and most crosstalk from the pair right next to them. Untwist the ends too far past the connector during termination and you've reopened the exact problem the twisting was solving.

Module 4

Cable types — wiring, not color

The cable type is defined by which pins carry which signal at each end, not by the jacket color. This is the checkpoint's favorite way to catch a guess.

Cable typeWiringUse case
Straight-throughSame pinout both endsDissimilar devices — PC↔switch, switch↔router
CrossoverTX/RX pairs swapped one endSimilar devices — switch↔switch, PC↔PC, router↔router
RolloverReverses every pinConsole cable — PC↔router/switch console port, management only
Auto-MDIX Modern switch ports auto-detect and adjust their pinout, so a straight-through cable often works where crossover was traditionally required. It doesn't remove the concept from the exam — it just removes it from most real cabling closets.

Applied

Worked examples

Think it through before you expand the answer.

Cable ID

Straight-through or crossover?

You're connecting a PC to a switch, and separately, connecting two switches together to extend a LAN. Which cable type does each connection need — and would it matter on a modern switch?

Show answer

PC to switch: dissimilar devices → straight-through. Switch to switch: similar devices → crossover, traditionally. On a modern switch with Auto-MDIX, both ports auto-detect and adjust, so a straight-through cable would likely work in either case. The underlying wiring concept — and the exam question — doesn't go away just because the hardware compensates for it.

Bandwidth vs. goodput

The overnight download

Your ISP advertises a 100 Mbps connection. A large file transfer overnight measures 80 Mbps of actual traffic on the line, but the download manager reports the file arrived at an effective rate of 65 Mbps. Name all three numbers.

Show answer

100 Mbps = bandwidth (the advertised, theoretical capacity). 80 Mbps = throughput (what was actually measured moving across the line). 65 Mbps = goodput (what the application actually got, after headers, retransmissions, and other overhead were subtracted out). Bandwidth ≥ throughput ≥ goodput, every time.

Copper

Why does the wire spiral?

A student notices that inside every Ethernet cable, the wire pairs are twisted rather than running straight and parallel. Why bother?

Show answer

Answer: Two wires in a pair carry opposite polarity signals. Twisted together, their magnetic fields largely cancel out both interference arriving from outside the cable (EMI/RFI — motors, fluorescent lights) and interference bleeding in from the pair right next to it (crosstalk). Different pairs are twisted at different rates specifically so their cancellation patterns don't line up with each other.

Fiber

SMF or MMF?

Your campus needs a link between two buildings three kilometers apart, and separately, a short run between two racks in the same data center. Which fiber type fits each job, and why not just use the more capable one everywhere?

Show answer

Building-to-building (3 km): single-mode fiber (SMF) — laser source, narrow core, built for long-haul distance. Rack-to-rack (short run): multimode fiber (MMF) — LED source, wider core, cheaper hardware, plenty of reach for a short distance. SMF isn't used everywhere because the laser transceivers cost more — there's no reason to pay for kilometers of range on a cable that's only ten feet long.

Layer 1 vs. everything else

The capture that shows nothing

A NOC tech starts a Wireshark capture to investigate why a student station can't reach the network at all — not slow, not intermittent, completely dark. The capture comes back empty. Is the capture broken, or is that the answer?

Show answer

That's the answer. Wireshark captures frames as they arrive on an interface. If Layer 1 never delivers a signal — a cut cable, a bad port, a disconnected patch — there's no frame to ever reach Layer 2, and nothing for the capture to show. The right tools here are a link light, a cable tester, or show interfaces on the switch — not a protocol analyzer. This is IR-01, The Saboteur: the one incident this semester with no packet-level trace at all.

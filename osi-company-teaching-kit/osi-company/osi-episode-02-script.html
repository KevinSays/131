EPISODE 02 — SHOOTING SCRIPT

THE IMPERSONATOR

Local Dispatch's address book doesn't lie — except when it's been told to. First appearance, first takedown.

COLD OPEN

A normal Tuesday. A workstation wants to talk to the gateway; the gateway wants to talk back. It's the most boring conversation in the building, and it succeeds several thousand times a minute, by design, without anyone checking anyone's ID.

ACT ONE — A NORMAL DAY

Establishing the healthy process before it gets abused.

Local Dispatch — Layer 2

Every device on this floor keeps a small notebook — the ARP table — mapping addresses to trucks. Filling it out is usually the least interesting part of anyone's day.

LOCAL DISPATCH

Who has 10.0.0.1? Tell 10.0.0.42.

GATEWAY

10.0.0.1 is at AA:BB:CC:00:00:01.

LOCAL DISPATCH

Noted. Filed. Moving on.

TEACHING NOTE — A normal ARP request/reply. Layer 2, unencrypted and unauthenticated by design — any device on the segment can answer, and nothing checks whether the answer is true.

Local Dispatch — Something's Off

Then, without being asked—

UNKNOWN (O.S.)

10.0.0.1 is at 11:22:33:44:55:66.

LOCAL DISPATCH

...Nobody asked. Filing it anyway — last answer wins.

TEACHING NOTE — A gratuitous ARP reply: an unsolicited announcement. ARP has no built-in way to verify a claim, so a device updates its table with whatever it hears last.

ACT TWO — THE IMPERSONATOR STRIKES

How they operate.

Reveal

THE IMPERSONATOR

Two lies, told to two different desks, and suddenly I'm not an outsider anymore — I'm the middleman nobody hired. Tell the workstation I'm the gateway. Tell the gateway I'm the workstation. Now every letter this floor sends walks past my desk first. I don't even have to open the envelopes. I can just hold them up to the light.

Two forged replies, sent to two different targets, each claiming to be the other. Neither victim sees anything wrong — their screens still work fine. That's what makes it work.

TEACHING NOTE — This is the attack: poisoning both the victim's and the gateway's ARP caches so traffic between them routes through the attacker — a man-in-the-middle position, achieved without touching a single cable.

MEANWHILE

Nothing on anyone's screen looks any different. That's the whole point.

ACT THREE — DAMAGE REPORT

What damage they can do.

FILED UNDER IR-02

Damage Assessment

  • Every packet between the workstation and the gateway now detours through an extra desk.
  • If Translation & Wardrobe wasn't already encrypting the traffic, the Eavesdropper reads all of it in the clear.
  • If a session token crosses that detour unprotected, the Impostor doesn't need to guess anything — it's just sitting there.
  • Nothing on the workstation's screen looks any different the entire time.
TEACHING NOTE — ARP spoofing is usually step one, not the whole attack — it's a positioning move that enables sniffing and session hijacking downstream. That's why it shows up first: everything else on the board gets easier once someone's in the middle.

ACT FOUR — THE NOC RESPONDS

What can defend against them. First appearance: the desk that never leaves.

The NOC — Introduction

Every floor has one desk that never leaves, never sleeps, and reads every ARP table in the building at once — comparing notes nobody else bothers to compare.

THE NOC

Two different MAC addresses claiming the same IP inside of four minutes. That's not a coincidence. That's a duplicate-IP alarm.

[Cross-references the switch port against the DHCP snooping binding table. The port claiming to be the gateway isn't the gateway's actual port.]

THE NOC

Got you. Dynamic ARP Inspection goes on every access port tonight. If a reply doesn't match what DHCP snooping already knows to be true, the switch drops it before it ever reaches a table. Gateway and file servers get static entries — you can't impersonate an address nobody has to ask about.

TEACHING NOTE — Dynamic ARP Inspection (DAI) validates ARP packets against a trusted DHCP snooping binding table and discards mismatches. Static ARP entries remove the trust-on-first-answer weakness for critical hosts, at the cost of manual upkeep.

ACT FIVE — REPAIR

How to repair the damage. Catching it is half the job.

The NOC's Checklist

  1. Isolate the offending switch port.
  2. Flush the poisoned ARP cache on every affected host — the lie doesn't leave on its own.
  3. Re-verify the gateway and DNS entries point where they should, not just that the table looks full again.
  4. Pull the logs for the window the Impersonator was active, and check whether the Eavesdropper or the Impostor made use of it.
  5. Rotate anything that crossed that detour unencrypted — credentials, session tokens, all of it.
  6. File the incident report. Turn DAI on everywhere it isn't already.

Front Desk — Closing Beat

FRONT DESK

Business as usual by 9am. Nobody upstairs ever found out there was an incident at all.

And that's the job. Nobody claps for the desk that never gets impersonated.

END OF EPISODE 02

Case file IR-02 closed. Six more incident reports still open on the board.
